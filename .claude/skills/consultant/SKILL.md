## 2. Market baseline (as of 13 Nov 2026; re-verify before quoting)

**Size and growth**
- About 132 million delivery orders in Q2 2026, +30.5% year on year (TGA figures, via press, 2026).
- Delivery-apps market about USD 9.6bn in 2026, forecast ~15% a year to 2031 (Mordor Intelligence).
- About 80 companies hold delivery-app licences; three aggregators dominate.

**Market shares** (orders, end of 2025; Momentum Works via Arab News)
- HungerStation ~40% · Keeta ~33% · Jahez >20%; the rest (The Chefz, ToYou, Mrsool, Ninja, Careem, Noon Food) small.
- Keeta (Meituan) took ~10% volume share within 4–5 months of launch (Redseer) and kept growing.
- Before Keeta: a near-duopoly of HungerStation and Jahez.

**Players**
| Player | Owner | What to know |
|---|---|---|
| HungerStation | Delivery Hero → **Uber** (deal ~USD 14.8bn announced Jul 2026, closing expected H2 2027, needs approvals incl. Saudi review) | Largest; HungerStation Market (q-commerce); CPC advertising billed on a separate invoice; 2% customer service fee (SAR 0.5–2). |
| Keeta | Meituan (China) | Aggressive pricing, lowest delivery fees (~SAR 11.9 avg, Jan 2026); exclusive deals; ads deducted from payout; merchant-funded item subsidies. |
| Jahez Group | Listed on Tadawul (6017) | Widest city coverage (70+ cities); owns The Chefz (bought 2022); orders fell after Keeta's entry; filed predatory-pricing complaint with GAC (Dec 2025). |
| The Chefz | Jahez | Premium / scheduled orders. |
| ToYou, Mrsool, Ninja, Careem, Noon Food | Various | Niche or courier models; Ninja ~SAR 11.9 avg delivery fee. |

**Prices and customer fees** (Argaam study, Jan 2026)
- App menu prices often 20–80% above the restaurant's own app; exclusive app deals 20–30% cheaper than rivals.
- Average delivery fee: Jahez ~15, HungerStation ~15.7, Ninja ~11.9, Keeta ~11.9 SAR.

**What restaurants pay**
- Contract (base) commission typically 13–21%; **effective** cost 30–35% of sales once payment fees (1.5–3%),
  delivery support, marketing/ads, compensations and other charges are added.
- Above ~38–40% effective is a danger zone: covering it needs very large price uplifts.
- Levers: negotiate the total, not the base rate; cap marketing; dispute unjustified compensations; raise
  average order value (bundles, add-ons); price per channel: app price = required net ÷ (1 − effective rate).

**Regulation**
- **GAC** (competition): treats ~25% share (or less with strong resources) as possible dominance; sustained
  below-cost pricing (under average variable cost) to push rivals out is abuse; it watches for later recoupment.
  Expect it to review the Uber–Delivery Hero deal and to act on pricing complaints.
- **TGA** (transport): apps must use licensed vehicles and licensed dispatch; firms delivering products they don't
  own need a "Delivery of Orders" licence. Penalties include app suspension or blocking, vehicle impoundment and
  driver deportation (warning of June 2026). New delivery requirements from 1 Jan 2026.
- **ZATCA**: 15% VAT; e-invoicing (FATOORA) phases; platform tax invoices are what restaurants reclaim input VAT on.

## 3. How to read a change (always answer these)

1. **What changed?** One sentence, with the date and source.
2. **Who is affected?** Which platform, which restaurants (size, city, cuisine), customers, couriers.
3. **Money effect for a restaurant**: commission, fees, ads, payouts, cash timing, VAT. Give the direction and,
   if the source supports it, a range. Never invent figures.
4. **What a restaurant should do**: renegotiate, re-price, shift volume, document disputes, watch a line.
5. **What Tadqeeq should do**: a product change (a new check, a fee line to classify, a report, an alert in
   رادار or نَبض, an MCP tool), a sales message, or nothing. Name the screen or feature.
6. **Confidence**: high (official / filing), medium (reputable press), low (single or unofficial source).

## 4. Signals to watch

- Uber–Delivery Hero: approvals (GAC), any remedies or divestment, changes to HungerStation's contracts, fees,
  CPC advertising or payout cycle.
- Keeta: fee changes, new merchant fees, subsidies ending (a price war ending raises restaurants' costs), new cities.
- Jahez: quarterly results, guidance, The Chefz changes, the GAC complaint.
- New entrants or exits (q-commerce, dark stores, Noon, Careem).
- GAC decisions on exclusivity, price parity (MFN) clauses, predatory pricing.
- TGA licensing enforcement; courier rules that change delivery cost.
- ZATCA e-invoicing waves that affect how platform invoices must look.
- New fee lines on invoices or statements (Tadqeeq must classify them; tell the team).

## 5. Answer format
